What Does Score Centering
Really Optimize?

Spoiler: one-step on-policy policy gradient on the sampler.

Bo DaiNovember 2026~10 min read
CONTENTS—TL;DR01Work in logit space02What direction does SC compute?03Stale samplers: bias and the accumulate correction04How this differs from off-policy correction05Takeaway
Sampler-side policy gradient

TL;DR

Score Centering Stabilizes Off-policy Reinforcement Learning (arXiv 2609.20807) explains score centering (SC) as canceling a "drift" term in the policy gradient under training-inference mismatch. A shorter reading: under a softmax policy, the centered score is exactly the sampler's own score in logit space. With a sampler synced every step, SC is therefore one-step on-policy policy gradient on the sampler, backpropagated through the trainer's Jacobian instead of the sampler's.

// § 01

Work in logit space

Let xxx be a prefix and yyy the next token. The trainer computes logits z(x)z(x)z(x) and the policy p(y∣x)=softmax(z(x))yp(y \mid x) = \mathrm{softmax}(z(x))_yp(y∣x)=softmax(z(x))y​. The sampler computes logits z~(x)\tilde z(x)z~(x) and the policy q(y∣x)=softmax(z~(x))yq(y \mid x) = \mathrm{softmax}(\tilde z(x))_yq(y∣x)=softmax(z~(x))y​. Tokens are sampled from qqq. We write p(⋅∣x)p(\cdot \mid x)p(⋅∣x) and q(⋅∣x)q(\cdot \mid x)q(⋅∣x) for the probability vectors and eye_yey​ for the one-hot vector of yyy.

Every gradient with respect to the parameters passes through the logits: ∇θ=(∂θz(x))⊤∇z(x)\nabla_\theta = (\partial_\theta z(x))^\top \nabla_{z(x)}∇θ​=(∂θ​z(x))⊤∇z(x)​. So we first look at SC in logit space, and bring in the Jacobian ∂θz(x)\partial_\theta z(x)∂θ​z(x) at the end.

In logit space, the trainer's score is ∇zlog⁡p(y∣x)=ey−p(⋅∣x)\nabla_{z} \log p(y \mid x) = e_y - p(\cdot \mid x)∇z​logp(y∣x)=ey​−p(⋅∣x), due to the softmax parametrization. SC subtracts its expectation under the sampler:

Ey′∼q(⋅∣x)[ey′−p(⋅∣x)]=q(⋅∣x)−p(⋅∣x),(ey−p(⋅∣x))−(q(⋅∣x)−p(⋅∣x))=ey−q(⋅∣x)=∇z~log⁡q(y∣x).\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{y' \sim q(\cdot \mid x)}\big[e_{y'} - p(\cdot \mid x)\big] &= q(\cdot \mid x) - p(\cdot \mid x), \\ \big(e_y - p(\cdot \mid x)\big) - \big(q(\cdot \mid x) - p(\cdot \mid x)\big) &= e_y - q(\cdot \mid x) = \nabla_{\tilde z} \log q(y \mid x). \end{aligned}Ey′∼q(⋅∣x)​[ey′​−p(⋅∣x)](ey​−p(⋅∣x))−(q(⋅∣x)−p(⋅∣x))​=q(⋅∣x)−p(⋅∣x),=ey​−q(⋅∣x)=∇z~​logq(y∣x).​

The trainer's distribution ppp cancels. The centered score is the sampler's own score. Nan Jiang made the same point for linear softmax policies: there, the correction simply computes the gradient under the sampling distribution.

What direction does SC compute?

A rollout τ\tauτ starts from a prompt x1∼Dx_1 \sim \mathcal{D}x1​∼D, samples yt∼q(⋅∣xt)y_t \sim q(\cdot \mid x_t)yt​∼q(⋅∣xt​), and extends the prefix xt+1=(xt,yt)x_{t+1} = (x_t, y_t)xt+1​=(xt​,yt​) until it ends; it receives reward R(τ)R(\tau)R(τ). The expected reward of the sampler is

J(q)=Ex1∼D,  yt∼q(⋅∣xt)[R(τ)].J(q) = \mathbb{E}_{x_1 \sim \mathcal{D},\; y_t \sim q(\cdot \mid x_t)}\big[R(\tau)\big].J(q)=Ex1​∼D,yt​∼q(⋅∣xt​)​[R(τ)].

The trainer's expected reward J(p)J(p)J(p) is defined the same way, with ppp in place of qqq. The advantage of token yyy at prefix xxx is

A(x,y)=Q(x,y)−Ey′∼q(⋅∣x)[Q(x,y′)],Q(x,y)=E[R(τ)∣xt=x, yt=y],A(x, y) = Q(x, y) - \mathbb{E}_{y' \sim q(\cdot \mid x)}\big[Q(x, y')\big], \qquad Q(x, y) = \mathbb{E}\big[R(\tau) \mid x_t = x,\ y_t = y\big],A(x,y)=Q(x,y)−Ey′∼q(⋅∣x)​[Q(x,y′)],Q(x,y)=E[R(τ)∣xt​=x, yt​=y],

where the expectation is over rollouts from qqq. In practice A(x,y)A(x, y)A(x,y) is estimated by R(τ)R(\tau)R(τ) minus a baseline, for example the mean reward of the rollouts for the same prompt (group centering), so all tokens of a rollout get the same estimate.

Let dq(x)=∑tPr⁡q(xt=x)d_q(x) = \sum_t \Pr_q(x_t = x)dq​(x)=∑t​Prq​(xt​=x) be the expected number of visits to prefix xxx under qqq. Treating each z~(x)\tilde z(x)z~(x) as a free parameter, the policy gradient theorem gives

∇z~(x)J(q)=dq(x)∑yq(y∣x) Q(x,y) (ey−q(⋅∣x))=dq(x)  Ey∼q(⋅∣x)[A(x,y) (ey−q(⋅∣x))],\nabla_{\tilde z(x)} J(q) = d_q(x) \sum_y q(y \mid x)\, Q(x, y)\, \big(e_y - q(\cdot \mid x)\big) = d_q(x)\; \mathbb{E}_{y \sim q(\cdot \mid x)}\Big[A(x, y)\, \big(e_y - q(\cdot \mid x)\big)\Big],∇z~(x)​J(q)=dq​(x)y∑​q(y∣x)Q(x,y)(ey​−q(⋅∣x))=dq​(x)Ey∼q(⋅∣x)​[A(x,y)(ey​−q(⋅∣x))],

where the baseline drops out because Ey∼q(⋅∣x)[ey−q(⋅∣x)]=0\mathbb{E}_{y \sim q(\cdot \mid x)}\big[e_y - q(\cdot \mid x)\big] = 0Ey∼q(⋅∣x)​[ey​−q(⋅∣x)]=0. Hence, for rollouts from qqq,

Eτ∼q[∑t1{xt=x} A(xt,yt) (eyt−q(⋅∣xt))]=∇z~(x)J(q).\mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim q}\Big[\sum_t \mathbb{1}\{x_t = x\}\, A(x_t, y_t)\, \big(e_{y_t} - q(\cdot \mid x_t)\big)\Big] = \nabla_{\tilde z(x)} J(q).Eτ∼q​[t∑​1{xt​=x}A(xt​,yt​)(eyt​​−q(⋅∣xt​))]=∇z~(x)​J(q).

So the centered score, weighted by the advantage, is the on-policy REINFORCE estimator of the sampler's logit-space policy gradient. The data come from qqq, so nothing needs correcting.

The trainer only maps this back to parameters:

∑tA(xt,yt) (∂θz(xt))⊤(eyt−q(⋅∣xt)).\sum_t A(x_t, y_t)\,\htmlClass{emphasize}{\big(\partial_\theta z(x_t)\big)}^\top \big(e_{y_t} - q(\cdot \mid x_t)\big).t∑​A(xt​,yt​)(∂θ​z(xt​))⊤(eyt​​−q(⋅∣xt​)).

An equivalent loss is −A(x,y) log⁡softmax(z(x)+sg[z~(x)−z(x)])y-A(x, y)\,\log \mathrm{softmax}\big(z(x) + \mathrm{sg}[\tilde z(x) - z(x)]\big)_y−A(x,y)logsoftmax(z(x)+sg[z~(x)−z(x)])y​: treat the logit gap between the two engines as a constant, take the forward pass from qqq and the gradient from ppp.

The exact policy gradient of the sampler would use the sampler's Jacobian ∂θz~(x)\partial_\theta \tilde z(x)∂θ​z~(x); SC uses the trainer's ∂θz(x)\partial_\theta z(x)∂θ​z(x).

  • In expectation, SC moves along ∑x(∂θz(x))⊤∇z~(x)J(q)\sum_x (\partial_\theta z(x))^\top \nabla_{\tilde z(x)} J(q)∑x​(∂θ​z(x))⊤∇z~(x)​J(q): the sampler's logit-space policy gradient, pulled back through the trainer's Jacobian. In general this is not the gradient of any objective.
  • It equals ∇θJ(q)=∑x(∂θz~(x))⊤∇z~(x)J(q)\nabla_\theta J(q) = \sum_x (\partial_\theta \tilde z(x))^\top \nabla_{\tilde z(x)} J(q)∇θ​J(q)=∑x​(∂θ​z~(x))⊤∇z~(x)​J(q) only when the two Jacobians agree. The gap is ∑x(∂θz(x)−∂θz~(x))⊤∇z~(x)J(q)\sum_x \big(\partial_\theta z(x) - \partial_\theta \tilde z(x)\big)^\top \nabla_{\tilde z(x)} J(q)∑x​(∂θ​z(x)−∂θ​z~(x))⊤∇z~(x)​J(q), and it comes only from the Jacobian swap.

So with a sampler synced every step, SC is one-step on-policy PG on the sampler. If the sampler is not synced every step, which is what happens in practice, the trainer moves away from it within the window, and taking gradients through the trainer's Jacobian at θk\theta_kθk​ instead of θ0\theta_0θ0​ introduces a bias (§03).

How this differs from off-policy correction

Vanilla on-policy PG samples from ppp itself. The other two methods use rollouts from qqq; what differs is which policy the update targets.

Vanilla on-policy PGA(x,y) (ey−p(⋅∣x))A(x, y)\,\big(e_y - p(\cdot \mid x)\big)A(x,y)(ey​−p(⋅∣x)), y∼py \sim py∼ppppnone

In PPO, the clip also acts as an approximate KL trust region.

Off-policy methods keep the trainer as the target and reweight the data toward it. SC does the opposite: it switches the target to the policy that generated the data, so the update is on-policy and needs no ratio or clipping. Two things make this possible in LLM RL. The sampler's logprobs are logged, so its score ey−q(⋅∣x)e_y - q(\cdot \mid x)ey​−q(⋅∣x) is computable. And the sampler shares parameters with the trainer, so an update to the sampler's logits can be carried to θ\thetaθ through ∂θz\partial_\theta z∂θ​z.

So the paper's framing does not fit. The SC update does not handle the distribution shift between trainer and sampler: it never reweights toward the trainer, and SC optimizes J(q)J(q)J(q), not J(p)J(p)J(p). It is one step of on-policy PG on the sampler, implemented over multiple trainer steps. The drift/signal decomposition is not essential to why SC works.

The bias therefore comes from somewhere else. Within a sync window the target is fixed: one on-policy PG step at θ0\theta_0θ0​. The per-step implementation takes each gradient through the trainer's Jacobian ∂θz(x)\partial_\theta z(x)∂θ​z(x) at θk\theta_kθk​ instead of at θ0\theta_0θ0​. This Jacobian difference is the whole bias (§03): it is zero for N=1N = 1N=1 and is removed by accumulating the gradients at θ0\theta_0θ0​.

// § 05

Takeaway

The method works and is cheap. The drift/signal framing is a detour: SC switches the target from the trainer to the sampler, and with a synced sampler it is one-step on-policy PG on the sampler. With a stale sampler, the paper's per-step updates add a bias that grows as N2N^2N2; accumulating the gradients over the window and updating once removes it at no extra cost.
